# What are smartphones used for besides calls and messages? #card

Smartphones are used for photos and videos, social media, email, banking, GPS navigation, gaming, online shopping, electronic payments, and controlling smart home devices.

# Main benefits of smartphones in society? #card

They improve communication, provide fast access to information, support education, enable online services, and increase productivity.

# Main problems of smartphone use? #card

They create privacy risks, distraction while driving, addiction, social isolation, cyberbullying, and constant surveillance.

# Why is location tracking dangerous? #card

It allows companies, hackers, or governments to know where users are, which can lead to stalking, profiling, or misuse.

# Why are phone cameras a privacy issue? #card

They allow secret recording in public and private places without consent.

# What is digital rudeness (phone etiquette)? #card

Using phones during conversations, classes, or meetings, disturbing others and reducing concentration.

# What is sexting and why is it risky? #card

Sending sexual images/messages; it can lead to blackmail, bullying, and legal problems.

# What is a kill switch? #card

A technical feature that allows companies to remotely disable software, delete files, or block devices.

# Main purpose of kill switches? #card

To remove malware, stolen devices, or illegal content for security reasons.

# Why are kill switches ethically problematic? #card

They reduce ownership, user control, privacy, and transparency.

# What happened in the Amazon Kindle case? #card

Amazon deleted illegal books remotely, showing that users don’t fully control digital purchases.

# What did Google’s Android kill switch prove? #card

Google can remotely remove apps from millions of devices.

# Main ethical issue in kill switch cases? #card

Users often do not know that companies have this power.

# What is social networking? #card

Online platforms for sharing content and communicating with others.

# Benefits of social networking? #card

Communication, activism, business marketing, fundraising, and collaboration.

# Main dangers of social networking? #card

Cyberbullying, stalking, misinformation, addiction, and fake identities.

# What is a socialbot? #card

An AI account that pretends to be human online to manipulate users.

# Why are socialbots dangerous? #card

They spread propaganda, scams, and fake opinions.

# Meaning of “On the Internet, nobody knows you’re a dog”? #card

People can easily hide their real identity online.

# What is privacy? #card

The right to control personal information and live without unnecessary surveillance.

# Three main meanings of privacy? #card

Freedom from intrusion, control of data, freedom from tracking.

# Why is online privacy fragile? #card

Data is stored forever, copied easily, and shared widely.

# What is personal information? #card

Any data that identifies a person, such as name, ID, location, email, or username.

# What is informed consent? #card

Users agreeing to data collection after being fully informed.

# Why is informed consent often weak? #card

Terms are long, complex, and rarely read.

# What is invisible data collection? #card

Gathering user data without awareness.

# Example of invisible data collection? #card

Tracking browsing habits through hidden software.

# What are cookies? #card

Small files storing browsing history and preferences.

# Benefits of cookies? #card

Personalized content and faster browsing.

# Risks of cookies? #card

Tracking behavior without consent.

# What are supercookies? #card

Advanced tracking tools that recreate deleted cookies.

# What is secondary use? #card

Using data for marketing, profiling, or selling without original consent.

# What is data mining? #card

Finding patterns in large databases.

# What is profiling? #card

Creating behavior models of users.

# Difference: opt-in vs opt-out? #card

Opt-in requires permission first; opt-out requires refusal later.

# Why is opt-in more ethical? #card

It respects user choice and autonomy.

# What is spam? #card

Unwanted mass emails sent for advertising or scams.

# Why is spam harmful? #card

Wastes time, spreads malware, and enables fraud.

# Do spam filters violate free speech? #card

No, because nobody is forced to receive messages.

# What is phishing? #card

Fake emails pretending to be legitimate to steal data.

# What is smishing? #card

Phishing via SMS.

# What is vishing? #card

Phishing via phone calls.

# What is pharming? #card

Redirecting users to fake websites.

# Why is identity theft dangerous? #card

It causes financial loss and legal problems.

# What is hacking? #card

Hacking is intentional and unauthorized access to computer systems, networks, or data.

# Phase 1 meaning of hacking (1960s–70s)? #card

Hacking meant creative programming and clever problem-solving.

# Phase 2 meaning of hacking (1970s–1990s)? #card

Hacking became associated with illegal system intrusion and password theft.

# Phase 3 meaning of hacking (1990s–present)? #card

Hacking involves global cybercrime, malware, data theft, and hacktivism.

# What is hacktivism? #card

Using hacking techniques for political or social protest.

# What is a denial-of-service attack (DoS)? #card

Overloading a system to make it unavailable.

# What are zombie computers? #card

Infected computers controlled remotely by attackers.

# Why is “harmless hacking” unethical? #card

It wastes resources, violates trust, and risks accidental damage.

# Example of harm from non-malicious hacking? #card

Companies must audit systems and may suffer service disruption.

# Ethical view of hacking? #card

It is digital trespassing and violates privacy and security.

# What is malware? #card

Malicious software such as viruses, worms, and trojans.

# What is a computer virus? #card

A program that copies itself and damages systems.

# What is a worm? #card

Self-spreading malware through networks.

# What is spyware? #card

Software that secretly collects user data.

# What is Artificial Intelligence (AI)? #card

Systems that perform tasks requiring human intelligence.

# What is the Turing Test? #card

A test to see if a computer can imitate human conversation.

# When does a machine pass the Turing Test? #card

When humans cannot distinguish it from a person.

# Main idea behind the Turing Test? #card

Intelligent behavior is more important than internal processes.

# Who criticized the Turing Test? #card

John Searle.

# What is the Chinese Room argument? #card

Computers manipulate symbols without real understanding.

# What does Searle claim about AI? #card

Machines simulate thinking but do not truly understand.

# Why is IBM Watson not “intelligent” for Searle? #card

It gives correct answers without consciousness.

# Main ethical concern about AI? #card

Job loss, bias, lack of accountability, and misuse.

# What is algorithmic bias? #card

When AI systems discriminate due to biased data.

# Why is transparency important in AI? #card

So users understand how decisions are made.

# What is automation bias? #card

Over-trusting machine decisions.

# Software Engineering Code: Client principle #card

# Software Engineering Code: Product principle #card

Ensure high-quality and safe systems.

# Software Engineering Code: Judgment principle #card

Maintain independence and integrity.

# Software Engineering Code: Management principle #card

Promote ethical development.

# Software Engineering Code: Profession principle #card

Protect professional reputation.

# Software Engineering Code: Colleagues principle #card

Support and respect coworkers.

# Software Engineering Code: Self principle #card

Lifelong learning and ethical growth.

# ACM principle: Contribute to society #card

Use computing to improve human life.

# ACM principle: Avoid harm #card

Prevent damage through careful design.

# ACM principle: Be honest #card

Report problems and limits truthfully.

# ACM principle: Avoid discrimination #card

Treat all users fairly.

# ACM principle: Honor property #card

Respect copyrights and patents.

# ACM principle: Give credit #card

Acknowledge others’ work.

# ACM principle: Respect privacy #card

Protect personal information.

# ACM principle: Honor confidentiality #card

Keep sensitive data secret.

# What is not copyrightable? #card

Legal sharing of content.

Copyleft license for free software.

Freedom with shared conditions.
